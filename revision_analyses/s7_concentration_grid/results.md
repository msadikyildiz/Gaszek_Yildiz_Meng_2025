## Bottom line — the Fig. 4 story holds at every concentration, with a
## consistent, biologically-coherent trend in the magnitude of non-additivity

### R² grows monotonically with epistatic order at every concentration

For every concentration of either drug, including higher-order epistatic
terms *strictly* improves the fit. The curve reaches R² ≥ 0.98 at
order 10 and R² ≥ 0.99 at order 11 at every condition, and asymptotes to
near-exactness at K = 13 (R² between 0.99925 and 0.99998 depending on
condition — the partial linear-regression basis is over-determined
relative to the 55,296-genotype library, so K = 13 is near-exact but not
bit-exact). No concentration shows a qualitative departure from the
main-text pattern.

### The *shape* of the R² curve is concentration-dependent in a biologically meaningful way

- **AMP**: Additive (order ≤ 1) prediction is strongest at intermediate
  concentrations (R² = 0.64 at 48.8 µg/mL) and degrades at both extremes.
  The lowest AMP R² at every order ≤ 10 is AMP 3.1 µg/mL (R² = 0.24 at

  signal; order 6 still reaches 0.53 even at the harshest concentration.
  This is the quantitative concentration signature of the ESBL-epistasis
  motif discussed throughout the manuscript.

### Pairwise-epistasis structure is *concentration-specific*, emerging through the selective window and weakening at both extremes

The pairwise-epistasis heatmaps (`pairwise_heatmap_{amp,azt}.png`,
column `biochemical_epistasis` in `data/pairwise_mean_fitness.csv`) show
that the *pattern* of which pairs of mutations interact is not
preserved across concentrations; it matures through the selective
window for each drug and weakens again in the sub-MIC and saturated
regimes. Pearson correlations of the flattened 19 × 19 matrix against
the main-text concentration panel quantify this explicitly:

| Reference | Other | Pearson r |
|---|---|---:|
| AMP 781 | AMP 195 | 0.20 |
| AMP 781 | AMP 48.8 | 0.24 |
| AMP 781 | AMP 12.2 | 0.24 |
| AMP 781 | AMP 3.1 | 0.29 |
| AZT 36 | AZT 0.44 | 0.11 |
| AZT 36 | AZT 1.33 | 0.39 |
| AZT 36 | AZT 4.0 | 0.73 |
| AZT 36 | AZT 12.0 | 0.76 |
| AZT 36 | AZT 108 | 0.55 |
| AZT 36 | AZT 324 | 0.14 |

For **AMP**, cross-concentration correlations are uniformly low
(r = 0.20–0.29) — which pairs interact changes substantially along the
dose range. The characteristic strong sign-flip interactions of the
main-text 781 µg/mL panel (max |ε| ≈ 2.0) do not appear below 195 µg/mL
(|ε| < 0.8 throughout), whereas AMP 3.1 µg/mL shows a distinct low-dose
pattern (max |ε| = 0.42, dominated by dynamic-range compression effects
rather than resistance epistasis).

For **AZT**, the pattern at 4–12 µg/mL is well-correlated with the
main-text 36 µg/mL panel (r = 0.73–0.76), but the two extremes (0.44
and 324 µg/mL) are essentially uncorrelated (r = 0.11 and 0.14). The
characteristic "ESBL cluster" (R164 × E104K × G238S × E240K) emerges
coherently in the 4–36 µg/mL window, peaks at 36 (max |ε| = 2.68), and
then softens at 108 (max |ε| = 2.33) and 324 (max |ε| = 1.46) as the
majority of the library reaches its selective extinction floor and
pairwise differences compress back toward zero.

This is the expected biology: pairwise epistasis is a
resolution-dependent property of the selection regime, visible only
where each individual mutation begins to matter and the library is not
yet dominated by extinction. The extended figure documents this
concentration-specificity for both drugs.

The concentration-specificity of pairwise-epistasis structure we observe
is not an idiosyncrasy of TEM-1 but reflects a phenomenon with an
established theoretical and empirical basis in the fitness-landscape
literature. The theoretical expectation was identified by Chevereau,
Dravecká, Batur, Guvenek, Ayhan, Toprak & Bollenbach (2015), who showed
across eight antibiotics that the steepness of the drug-specific
dose-response curve (Hill coefficient) is the primary determinant of
variation in the distribution of fitness effects — a mechanism that
predicts that different concentrations of a single drug, sitting at
different slopes of that drug's own Hill curve, will exhibit different
epistatic structures. Ogbunugafor et al. (2016) provided the first
empirical confirmation of this prediction within a single drug, using
fitness landscapes of *Plasmodium falciparum* dihydrofolate reductase
across a pyrimethamine concentration gradient: rank orderings of alleles,
accessible evolutionary pathways, and cross-concentration landscape
correlations all shifted substantially with drug dose, with Pearson R²
between landscapes at different concentrations declining toward
non-significance at extreme concentrations. Díaz-Colunga, Sanchez &
Ogbunugafor (2023) extended this framework in the same DHFR system by
demonstrating that the underlying mechanism is the concentration-dependent
modification of specific gene-by-gene pairwise interactions, not merely
a global rescaling of fitness, concluding that epistasis predictions are
only interpretable when the selective environment is specified. In a
β-lactamase-family system directly analogous to ours, Chen, Fowler &
Tokuriki (2022) showed that the direction and magnitude of epistasis in
VIM-2 under ampicillin are concentration-dependent across a 64-fold dose
range. Our data add to this body of evidence at unprecedented resolution
within TEM-1: a fully combinatorial 55,296-genotype library sampled
across 12 drug-by-concentration conditions directly measures how
pairwise-epistasis topography reorganises along each drug's dose-response
curve — empirically confirming within-drug the Hill-curve prediction of
Chevereau et al. (2015). For AZT, the pairwise-epistasis pattern is
reproducible within the 4–36 µg/mL selective window (cross-concentration
r = 0.73–0.76) and decorrelates sharply outside it (r = 0.11–0.14); for
AMP the pattern reorganises substantially across the entire measured
dose range (r = 0.20–0.29). All pairwise-interaction claims in this
manuscript are therefore presented with their concentration annotation,
and the main-text concentrations (AMP 781 µg/mL, AZT 36 µg/mL) were
chosen to sit inside each drug's selective window by design. See
`literature_backing.md` for the full citation record.

### Measured-vs-predicted density panels preserve the main-text interpretation

Visually (`pred_vs_measured_{amp,azt}.png`), every row shows the same
textbook progression of the density cloud tightening toward the y = x
diagonal as K increases. At every concentration, K = 1 leaves a wide
off-diagonal tail of true-positive resistant variants that the additive
model under-predicts; K = 3 cuts this tail substantially; K = 6 nearly
eliminates it. The asymmetric under-prediction at low K (bottom-right of
each panel: high measured fitness, low predicted fitness) is the visual
fingerprint of ESBL-like epistasis and is present at every AZT
concentration ≥ 4 µg/mL.

## Where the pattern "bends"

Two concentrations deserve explicit annotation in the extended figure
caption; neither contradicts the manuscript's claims:

- **AMP 3.1 µg/mL.** This is a sub-MIC condition — all variants cluster
  in a narrow fitness band (∼2.5 to 3.8 log₁₀ AUC). R² at order 1 is
  0.24 — the lowest among all AMP conditions — but the low figure is
  dominated by the *small dynamic range of measured fitness* (i.e. a
  small R² denominator), not by failure of the model. Order-6 R² rises
  to 0.61 and order-10 to 0.98, consistent with a compressed-variance
  regime rather than with genuinely high-order epistasis.
- **AZT 324 µg/mL.** This is the most-selective AZT condition. Order-1
  R² = 0.06 and order-2 R² = 0.10. Adding order 3 still only reaches
  R² = 0.15 — this is the highest-order-epistasis regime measured in the
  manuscript and is a positive confirmation of the paper's thesis: the
  ESBL-like resistance phenotype requires coordinated multi-residue
  substitutions that pairwise terms alone cannot reconstruct. Order-6
  R² is still a respectable 0.53 at this condition; the full-order
  (K = 13) fit reaches R² = 0.9993.

## Methodological notes

- **Predictions are NOT re-computed** from scratch. We read the
  `Fitness_predicted for order K` columns already stored in
  `data/processed/Epistasis_Combined.parquet`. These columns were
  produced by `src/utils/calculate_epistasis.py`
  (`Epistasis_LG_partial_calculate`), which fits a single full-data
  (no train/test split) partial linear regression per (drug,
  concentration) and then zeroes out all interaction terms above
  maximum order K. The library has 55,296 genotypes across 13
  positions with variable state counts per position, so the
  full-order (K = 13) fit is asymptotically near-exact (R² between
  0.99925 and 0.99998 depending on condition) rather than bit-exact.
  Matching this convention ensures the new extended figures are
  directly comparable to Figure 4 in the main text.
- **R² is computed as the Pearson correlation squared** between measured
  and predicted fitness, matching the formula used for Fig. 4 D in
  `src/05_epistasis_figures.ipynb` (cell 16). RMSD is also reported in
  `data/regression_r2_by_order.csv` for completeness.
- **Pairwise heatmaps use the `Biochemical Definition` column** (the
  manuscript's choice for Fig. 5 E/F). Single mutants fill the first
  row/column of the 19×19 matrix (i.e. "WT × mut"); double mutants fill
  the off-diagonal block. Per-drug symmetric colour limits (±3 for AMP,
  ±4 for AZT) reproduce the main-text figure exactly.
- **AMP uses greyscale, AZT uses RdPu** for the density panels, matching
  the manuscript's drug-colour convention (AMP = grey, AZT = hotpink).

## Library-invariance hardening (AMP 781 µg/mL)

To pre-empt a follow-up concern about the sequencing-floor fraction at the
main-text AMP concentration, we ran an invariance test in
`library_invariance/`: recomputing every Fig 4 / Fig 5 analysis at AMP
781 µg/mL with and without the 14.9 % of the landscape subset flagged as
`majority_low` (per-replicate read sum across 4 timepoints < 10 in ≥ 2/3
replicates — same rule used in the revised Fig S3). The biochemical
pairwise-epistasis matrix (Fig 5 E/F view) is numerically identical
between full and clean (r = 1.0000); the R²-vs-order curve differs by at
most ΔR² = 0.05 at any K with the clean subset systematically higher; and
a LightGBM retrain on the clean subset improves test RMSD from 0.46 to
0.37 while preserving the SHAP top-driver ranking (ρ = 0.87; R244S,
R244C, A237T, L21P unchanged). Every Fig 4 / Fig 5 claim at AMP 781 is
therefore invariant to exclusion of the sequencing-floor tail — and in
fact tightens under it. See `library_invariance/results.md` for the full
paragraph and numbers. Also worth noting: the "32 %" low-read figure is
from the raw 70k-row read-count CSV; the landscape subset that Fig 4/5
actually analyses has only 14.9 % majority-low at AMP 781.
